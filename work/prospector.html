Business Problem

What was costing the business

All our lead generation depended on paid inbound sources, comparison sites that charge a premium for each lead. We had no say over how many leads came in, who they were, or when.

My Goal

What I set out to do

I wanted an outbound channel that didn't depend on paid lead providers. I built it as a proper sales pipeline, not a plain contact list, so I could test it, tweak it and expand it over time. I didn't want a one-off scraper.

Architecture

How it’s built

Lead discovery uses open map data instead of a paid source. An AI model reads each prospect's website and drafts a tailored cold email. A pipeline then tracks each prospect through a five-stage chase timeline that follows how a real sales process moves, instead of sending everything in one blast.

Implementation

The details that mattered

I built it to run on my own computer, as my own project, separate from the company's internal tools. It's still being developed and isn't a finished, deployed system.

Business Outcome

What changed

At the moment Prospector gives me an outbound lead channel that doesn't rely on paid comparison sites, with a pipeline built around how sales conversations actually go. It's early. I'm not claiming a measured result yet, and I'd rather say so than overstate it.

Lessons Learned

What I took from it

Free, open data can replace paid sources for finding leads if you're willing to build the enrichment layer yourself. The harder problem wasn't finding prospects. It was writing outreach that doesn't sound generic.

Engineering Decisions

Why OpenStreetMap instead of a paid data provider?
It removes the per-lead cost completely. The trade-off is building the enrichment layer yourself, which turned out to be the more interesting problem anyway.
